Philosophy

A quiet frame. The material comes first.

Start with the material

Structure follows the information. Long text needs readable measure; dense archives need efficient scanning; tools need clear state and controls. Reuse the visual language, not a universal page template.

Keep the interface subordinate

  • Prefer text, whitespace, and alignment before containers or decoration.
  • Use serif for reading, sans serif for interface and metadata, and monospace only for values, identifiers, shortcuts, and compact status.
  • Keep the palette neutral with one restrained blue for links, focus, selection, and meaningful state.
  • Use hairline rules to explain structure. Avoid boxes, shadows, gradients, and animation unless the material needs a real boundary, depth cue, or transition.
  • Keep controls square, compact, plainly labeled, and close to the content they affect. Equivalent dropdowns share one visual and keyboard grammar; remove duplicate controls that do the same job in the same context.
  • Let titles be calm and close to the material they govern.

Make hierarchy skimmable

A long page should work at several depths: title and dateline, section headings, short summaries, primary material, then disclosed detail. Put context in a margin when room permits and return it to the reading flow on narrow screens.

Whitespace must earn its place. Use margins for useful context or comparison, not unexplained section numbers or empty hero space. Tighten oversized gaps and unbalanced columns while retaining readable measure and clear separation.

Major changes of subject may use one ruled ornament. Its symbol can respond to the material, but its weight stays quiet. A transition has one owner: adjacent sections and disclosures give up their own rules so punctuation never becomes an accidental stack.

A puzzle page uses one quiet PNG-centered divider at the start of the main puzzle unit, after complete rules and worked examples. Keep its lead-in, solve links, and grids together below the line. Choose the boundary by meaning, not the first image; the line follows the layout without separating a puzzle from its own introduction.

Earn every feature

Search, filtering, sorting, pagination, drawers, and dialogs belong only when they make material easier to find, read, compare, or operate. The simple case should remain simple as a dataset grows.

Choose one collection-navigation model: compact paginated rows in normal page flow with details on demand, or a true matrix in a named overflow region. Do not combine pagination with an internally scrolling two-axis table. Show sort direction in headings; explain primary and secondary sorting in advanced controls rather than unexplained digits.

Share foundations, preserve identity

The theme owns palette, type roles, focus behavior, accessibility helpers, standard shells, transition grammar, and the color-mode contract. Each site keeps the layouts and visualizations its subject genuinely requires.

Each masthead pairs its text title with a small, relevant transparent PNG, never a Unicode glyph or emoji. Related marks remain quiet and legible on paper and charcoal; the title, not the decorative image, supplies the name. Favicons retain their separate identity.

Accessibility is part of the style

Pages work with a keyboard, retain visible focus, expose useful labels and live status, respect reduced motion, remain legible in light and dark modes, and simplify rather than merely shrink on narrow screens.

Test the ends of the scale

Review empty and realistic states, narrow and wide viewports, both color modes, keyboard focus, and the largest plausible collection. Minimalism succeeds when there is less interface to manage without losing capability.
